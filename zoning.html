Zoning Comparison: Rules vs. What’s Built

How lots in Beverly's two multifamily districts, RHD and RMD, compare to each district's lot size, frontage, and setback requirements.

On this page
Overview What the Rules Require What's on the Ground Look Up a Lot Methodology & Limitations

Overview

Beverly's Zoning Ordinance sets minimum dimensions for every lot in the RHD (Multifamily High Density) and RMD (Multifamily Medium Density) districts: how large a lot must be, how much of it must face a street, and how far a building must sit from each lot line. Many of the buildings in these districts were built before these rules existed.

This page compares each lot against those requirements using the City's own map data. The results are estimates from GIS measurements, not surveys, and they do not indicate whether any property is in violation of zoning. See "What nonconforming means" below.

District

What the Rules Require

RHD requirements come from § 300-37 of the Zoning Ordinance. 412 of the district's 414 lots are also inside the MBTA Communities Overlay District (§ 300-140), which keeps the same frontage, setback, and height rules but changes how lot area is calculated.

RMD requirements come from § 300-36 of the Zoning Ordinance. 171 of the district's 694 lots are also inside the MBTA Communities Overlay District (§ 300-140), which keeps the same frontage, setback, and height rules but replaces the per-unit lot area requirement with a cap of eight units per lot.

What's on the Ground

Open Beverly measured 414 RHD lots and the principal building on 394 of them. Measurements within 3 feet of a requirement are shown separately, because building footprints in the City's data include roof overhangs and parcel lines are drawn from assessor maps, not surveys.

12 of the 394 measured lots meet or come within 3 feet of all five requirements. 6 lots in the MBTA overlay have more than nine units.

Parking. RHD and RMD require two off-street parking spaces per dwelling unit (§ 300-59). Based on mapped driveways, parking areas, and garages recorded by the Assessor, Open Beverly estimates that between 46% and 57% of RHD lots have enough off-street space to meet that requirement. This estimate is shown for the district only, not for individual lots, because paved area does not translate reliably into usable parking spaces. The requirement applies to new or expanded uses; existing parking shortfalls may continue under § 300-85.

For comparison, the City's Planning Department estimated in 2024 that "approximately 80%" of parcels in the RHD and RMD districts combined were nonconforming. By Open Beverly's measurements, 98% of the 1,053 measured lots in the two districts fall clearly short of at least one requirement, and 82% of all lots fall short on lot area alone. The City has not published its method, so the figures are not directly comparable.

What nonconforming means

A lot or building that does not meet current dimensional rules is called nonconforming. Under Massachusetts law (MGL Chapter 40A, § 6), structures that were lawfully built before a zoning rule took effect may remain. Changes or additions to a nonconforming structure may require a finding or special permit from the Zoning Board of Appeals. This page does not assess whether any lot has received a variance or special permit.

Methodology & Limitations

How these estimates were made

  • Lots: City of Beverly GIS parcel layer. Condominium records that share one lot were combined, giving 414 RHD lots and 694 RMD lots.
  • Buildings: City of Beverly GIS roofprint layer. Only the largest building on each lot was measured, because accessory buildings such as sheds and garages have separate rules.
  • Frontage: the longest unbroken lot edge along a single street. On corner lots, each street is measured separately.
  • Corner lots: the ordinance allows either street to be designated the front yard (Article II, "Yard, front"). Open Beverly tested each street and used the more favorable result.
  • Tolerance: setbacks are measured to the foundation (Article II, "Setback"), while roofprints include roof overhangs. Measurements within 3 feet of a requirement, or within 5% for lot area, are shown as too close to call.
  • Parking: mapped driveway and parking area on each lot, converted to spaces using the ordinance's 9-by-18-foot stall (§ 300-64), with and without room for drive aisles, plus garages recorded by the Assessor. Reported as a range for each district.
  • RMD rear setback: the City's MBTA Communities summary page lists the current RMD rear setback as 10 feet. The Zoning Ordinance text (§ 300-36) specifies 20 feet, which is used here.
  • Not measured: building height, which the City's data does not include, and the separate rules for semidetached dwellings.
  • Zoning: Zoning Ordinance text as amended through June 15, 2026 (Ord. No. 119-2026). GIS data accessed September 28, 2026.
