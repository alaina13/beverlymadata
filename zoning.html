Zoning Comparison: Rules vs. What’s Built

How lots in Beverly's Multifamily High Density (RHD) district compare to the district's lot size, frontage, and setback requirements.

On this page

Overview

Beverly's Zoning Ordinance sets minimum dimensions for every lot in the RHD district: how large a lot must be, how much of it must face a street, and how far a building must sit from each lot line. Many of the buildings in the district were built before these rules existed.

This page compares each RHD lot against those requirements using the City's own map data. The results are estimates from GIS measurements, not surveys, and they do not indicate whether any property is in violation of zoning. See "What nonconforming means" below.

What the Rules Require

RHD requirements come from § 300-37 of the Zoning Ordinance. 412 of the district's 414 lots are also inside the MBTA Communities Overlay District (§ 300-140), which keeps the same frontage, setback, and height rules but changes how lot area is calculated.

What's on the Ground

Open Beverly measured 414 RHD lots and the principal building on 394 of them. Measurements within 3 feet of a requirement are shown separately, because building footprints in the City's data include roof overhangs and parcel lines are drawn from assessor maps, not surveys.

11 of the 394 measured lots meet or come within 3 feet of all five requirements. 6 lots in the MBTA overlay have more than nine units.

For comparison, the City's Planning Department estimated in 2024 that "approximately 80%" of parcels in the RHD and RMD districts combined were nonconforming. The City's figure covers a different set of lots, and its method is not published, so the two figures are not directly comparable.

What nonconforming means

A lot or building that does not meet current dimensional rules is called nonconforming. Under Massachusetts law (MGL Chapter 40A, § 6), structures that were lawfully built before a zoning rule took effect may remain. Changes or additions to a nonconforming structure may require a finding or special permit from the Zoning Board of Appeals. This page does not assess whether any lot has received a variance or special permit.

Methodology & Limitations

How these estimates were made

  • Lots: City of Beverly GIS parcel layer. Condominium records that share one lot were combined, giving 414 lots.
  • Buildings: City of Beverly GIS roofprint layer. Only the largest building on each lot was measured, because accessory buildings such as sheds and garages have separate rules.
  • Frontage: the longest unbroken lot edge along a single street.
  • Corner lots: the ordinance allows either street to be designated the front yard (Article II, "Yard, front"). Open Beverly tested each street and used the more favorable result.
  • Tolerance: setbacks are measured to the foundation (Article II, "Setback"), while roofprints include roof overhangs. Measurements within 3 feet of a requirement, or within 5% for lot area, are shown as too close to call.
  • Not measured: building height, which the City's data does not include, and the separate rules for semidetached dwellings.
  • Zoning: Zoning Ordinance text as amended through June 15, 2026 (Ord. No. 119-2026). GIS data accessed September 28, 2026.
