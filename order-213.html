Order 213: Human Rights Committee Funding

What the Beverly City Council discussed and voted on at its November 9, 2026 meeting.

On this page
Overview Intended Use of the $20,000 Public Comment Council Discussion Votes Notes & Limitations

Overview

Order 213 asked the mayor to raise and appropriate $20,000 for the Beverly Human Rights Committee to support its work. Councilor Scott Houseman sponsored it. The Finance and Property subcommittee sent it to the full council for discussion without a recommendation.

The vote was a request to the mayor and did not itself appropriate funds. The City Solicitor said the council could not set the required public hearing on its own, and that the mayor would need to submit a supplemental appropriation. Councilor Houseman said the mayor had authorized him to tell the council that, if it passed Order 213 as written, the mayor would submit an order to raise and appropriate the $20,000.

The order followed the council's June 2026 budget vote, which eliminated the DEIB director position. Speakers in the discussion described that vote as 5-4. The order passed 5-3. An amendment to request about $62,120 instead, the amount originally proposed for the position in the FY2027 budget, failed 4-4.

Councilor Houseman said the order needed a vote on November 9 so that a public hearing could be set for December 7, 2026, before the tax rate is set. Councilor Spang noted that a later appropriation could still come from free cash. Councilor Houseman agreed and said the order was an attempt to do it within the budget.

Intended Use of the $20,000

Order 213 does not list specific uses. It says the funds are to support the Human Rights Committee's work.

Eric Knutson, chair of the Human Rights Committee, described the committee's intent in response to a question from Councilor Spang:

The committee would host and support events with organizations that already do human rights or related work, and would identify that work as human rights work. Events the city already holds include the pride flag raising, the Martin Luther King Jr. breakfast, and a Juneteenth event. He also described opening events to residents who do not normally attend, including residents without internet access, transportation, or childcare. He said events should be equitable, accessible, and not divisive, and should bring different parts of the community together.

Mr. Knutson said he was still reviewing the City Solicitor's guidance on how the funds may be spent and had not yet met with his committee about specific events. No event count or budget breakdown was given.

On administration, Councilor Houseman said the committee would propose ideas to the mayor, who would approve or decline them, and that the mayor holds final authority under the City Charter. The City Solicitor said the spending would be subject to state procurement law and the normal review process, with the committee working with finance staff as other departments do. Councilor St. Hilaire said that in practice the mayor would have full authority over how the money is spent.

Public Comment

Eric Knutson, Human Rights Committee chair (Ward 4)

Mr. Knutson spoke in support of Order 213 during public comment. He cited a Beverly resident who vandalized pride symbols in Salem, an incarcerated Beverly resident accused of making violent threats against Jewish women who is expected to return to the community, and a survey in which a quarter of respondents in an active bystander pilot program said they had witnessed bias, harassment, or discrimination in Beverly in the past year. He said the Human Rights Committee cannot replace the DEIB director, but that it can bring residents together through community events. He was later invited by Councilor Houseman to speak again during the council discussion (see above).

The Council President said there was no time for general public comment on this item and that Mr. Knutson spoke because he was representing the committee.

Council Discussion

Statements are summarized from the meeting video. The councilors are listed roughly in the order they first spoke.

Councilor Scott Houseman (sponsor)

He said he wrote the order over the summer after the June budget vote. He raised the subcommittee suggestion to fund the position at the original FY2027 amount for discussion. He described Order 213 as a different model for DEI work: community-facing events run by the committee, as opposed to a salaried position in the mayor's office. He cited the projected deficit of about $1.56 million and said he expected staffing cuts. He called the order a pilot, and said it addressed community events and not the administration's internal staffing.

Councilor Mullady

He moved the amendment to request the original FY2027 amount, and said he intended for the mayor's office to be able to bring the position back, restructured if needed. He said the June 5-4 vote showed how close the council was. He asked whether the council would eliminate a whole department over dissatisfaction with a department head. He said he was concerned about shifting responsibility to volunteers, comparing it to families and parent-teacher organizations covering classroom supplies. He said the additional amount was small relative to the deficit. He said he would not support Order 213 as written, and that the discussion could have happened in June.

Councilor St. Hilaire (Vice President)

He asked the City Solicitor about the council's authority to pass the spending. He said the money for the position had been mismanaged and that more than $1 million had been spent on DEI across the schools and the municipal side without tangible results. He noted that the committee recently needed an ordinance change because it could not hold a quorum, and that Mr. Knutson is a volunteer chair and not a department head. He asked what the mayor, chief of staff, HR director, and constituent services director are doing on DEI, and asked to hear from the administration. On the original order, he said the mayor would have full authority over how the money is spent.

Councilor Feldman (Finance and Property chair)

She said her June vote reflected frustration with the administration and with how the position was structured. She said she wants to fund the work but was not comfortable restoring the position in its June form on the current timeline, and would like to see it return under HR in a different form in the next budget cycle. She described the $20,000 as an interim step. She said the mayor can request a free cash transfer at any time, and that the order signals where the council would like funding to go.

Councilor Crowley

He asked whether the larger amount was to restore the position. He said he agreed with Councilors Feldman and St. Hilaire about the lack of results from the earlier position, could not support the full amount, and was open to the lesser one. Later, he summarized that passing the order approves no money and only sends a request back to the mayor, who would need to return to the council. He moved to call the vote.

Councilor Spang

He asked about the urgency of voting that night, what the $20,000 would support, and, procedurally, what the amendment would do. The Council President answered the last question: the council cannot put money back into the budget directly, so passing the amendment would request that the mayor consider raising and appropriating up to that amount.

Other councilors

One councilor (name not clear in the transcript) said they had proposed 12 budget amendments in June to cut $2.7 million, that cutting the DEI director was the only one that passed, and that they regretted it sent a bad message. They said that with another deficit ahead, asking the mayor to spend more did not make sense. Another councilor asked who would oversee the money, and later asked who would be responsible for administering it and how it would be spent.

Council President

After swapping seats with the Vice President to speak on the amendment, the Council President said she had voted in June against eliminating the position, and that the cut was less than one half of one percent of the budget. She said residents told her the cut made them fear that Beverly was backing away from inclusion. She said the difference between the original amount and $20,000 was small enough that she did not see the question as financial, and that the city should not rely only on volunteers. She said she supported the amendment.

Votes

Votes on Order 213 and its amendment, November 9, 2026
Motion Result
Amendment: request about $62,120 (the original FY2027 amount for the DEIB director position) Failed 4-4
Order 213: request the mayor to raise and appropriate $20,000 for the Human Rights Committee Approved 5-3

Both votes were taken by voice. The meeting video does not show how each councilor voted.
